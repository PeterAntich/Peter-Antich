I work on the sources of meaning in perceptual and embodied life.

Associate Professor of Philosophy, Dominican University of New York.

My research addresses questions in the philosophy of perception and epistemology through phenomenology, but it is pluralistic in method: I treat phenomenology as a resource enriched by dialogue with analytic philosophy, critical theory, and the history of philosophy. I test phenomenological claims against concrete cases — such as dementia, color vision, developmental psychology, and the perceptual dimensions of racism.

In progress

Monograph
Representation and Awareness: A Phenomenological Sketch. Under contract with Routledge. It argues that perceptual awareness lacks a vehicle, and that no satisfactory account of phenomenal character lets perception be both representational and direct — pointing toward a relational view that is nonetheless not naïve realism.

Volume

Book
A study of perceptual categorization, drawing on Stein, Merleau-Ponty, and Levinas, in which perceptual categories are neither fixed natural kinds nor projections but emerge through the relation between perceptual sense and embodied habituation.
