Phenomenology · Perception · Epistemology

I work on where meaning comes from in perceptual and embodied life.

Associate Professor of Philosophy,
Dominican University of New York.

My research addresses questions in the philosophy of perception and epistemology through phenomenology, but it is pluralistic in method: I treat phenomenology as a resource enriched by dialogue with analytic philosophy, critical theory, and the history of philosophy. I test phenomenological claims against concrete cases — dementia, color vision, developmental psychology, and the perceptual dimensions of racism.
