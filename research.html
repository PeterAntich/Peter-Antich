Research

Two threads run through this work. The first is epistemic: what Merleau-Ponty calls motivation names a ground of knowledge distinct from both reason and natural causality, and reopens debates about perceptual justification, foundationalism, and skepticism. The second is intentional: perception has a content — perceptual sense — irreducible to conceptual and sensory content alike, which neither realism nor representationalism can accommodate. Recent work presses the anti-representationalist argument toward a relational account of awareness, and extends the question of perceptual content to categorization.
